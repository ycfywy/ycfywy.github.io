最近总听到一个词：**卡了**。

电脑转了半天，点什么都没反应，卡了。打游戏的时候，人物突然停在原地，按键按了好几遍，屏幕还是不动，也卡了。

以前说这个词，我想到的基本都是设备或者游戏。现在再听到它，却觉得它好像也能形容人。不是身体动不了，而是生活走到某个地方，突然不知道该往哪里走了。

我最近就有点这种感觉。

## 大家好像都在往 AI 跑

这两年，只要聊到科技、工作和未来，基本绕不开 AI。新模型一个接一个，新闻里不是哪个公司投了多少钱，就是哪个产品又更新了。每天打开手机，都感觉世界在加速。

但世界加速，和我知道自己该做什么，好像是两回事。

今年看就业，给我的一个感觉是，半导体、材料以及一些硬件相关的方向，似乎比以前更受关注了。大家都在建 AI 数据中心，要买 GPU、芯片、内存、存储设备，还要配服务器、网络和电力。钱往这些地方流，相关厂商自然也能受益。

当然，这只是我从新闻和身边信息里看到的一个趋势，不是说所有硬件岗位都好找，也不是说软件公司把钱全部拿去买了设备。只是当大量资金和资源都在追着算力跑的时候，我很难不去想：留给其他方向的机会，还有多少？

## AI 很热闹，但新机会在哪里？

我自己也在用 AI，尤其是写代码的时候。查问题、补代码、改个小工具，它确实能帮不少忙。有些以前要折腾半天的事情，现在可能一会儿就有了一个能跑的版本。

但除了这些，我总觉得，AI 在很多商业场景里还没有真正找到特别成熟、特别清晰的用法。不是说完全没有应用，而是我还没有看到那么多让人觉得“原来以前根本没有这种生活方式”的东西。

互联网刚兴起的时候，可能也只是把一些信息搬到网上，像电子黄页一样，查查餐厅电话、找找联系方式。后来才慢慢有了网上购物、外卖、网约车、数字银行、线上娱乐。它们改变了很多人的生活，也带来了新的商业模式和工作岗位。

AI 以后会不会走出类似的路？我不知道。也许现在还太早，很多东西尚未出现。但至少在我目前能看到的范围里，硬件和算力的投入已经很大，真正普遍的新业态却还没有那么清楚。两者之间的落差，让人有点不踏实。

## 快了，不代表好了

我对 AI 还有一个很强烈的感受：它现在更多是在提高效率，而不是提高工作质量。至少就我自己的体验来说，**我不觉得它提高了工作质量**。

比如写一段代码，它能很快给我一个答案。但这个答案是不是适合我的问题，有没有隐藏的错误，能不能长期使用，最后还是得自己判断。它可以让我更快地拿到一个看起来像样的结果，却不能保证这个结果真的好。

写东西也是一样。几秒钟生成一大段文字很容易，但里面有没有真实的观察，有没有准确的判断，是不是作者真正想说的话，那就是另一回事了。

我不是否认 AI 有用。我每天都能感受到它有用。只是有时候，大家把它说得太聪明了，好像它什么都懂、什么都能做。实际用久了才发现，它也会一本正经地说错话，也会在一些看似简单的地方犯错。它有能力，但也有明显的边界。我觉得现在的热潮里多少有些泡沫，只是泡沫有多大，谁也说不准。

## 真正让我卡住的，是自己的下一步

说了这么多行业，其实绕到最后，还是绕回了自己。

我是一个学生。眼前有毕业的问题，后面还有工作的问题。导师的一些决策、自己做过的一些选择，还有个人能力上的不足，混在一起，很难简单地说清楚到底是哪一件事造成了现在的局面。

我会担心自己能不能顺利毕业。想到毕业之后，又不知道下一步应该做什么。要继续做现在的方向，还是换一个方向？要准备实习、补基础，还是先把眼前的研究做好？每件事好像都有道理，每件事又都需要时间。

最难受的不是忙，而是不知道该把劲往哪里使。

有时候我也想，既然焦虑，那就努力一点吧。可坐下来以后，又会问自己：努力什么？今天花时间做的事情，真的能帮我往前走吗？如果方向本身就不确定，再努力会不会只是绕了一大圈？

于是一天过去了，事情做了一些，心里却没有踏实多少。晚上躺下，脑子里还在想论文、毕业、找工作，想那些暂时没有答案的问题。第二天醒来，还是得接着面对。

## 卡住的时候

我觉得“卡了”这个词，说的就是这种状态。

表面上看，日子还在继续，每天也不是完全没有事情做。别人问起来，好像还能说自己最近在忙什么。可只有自己知道，有些问题一直悬在那里，想了很久，还是不知道怎么解决。看似在往前走，实际上却觉得自己已经没招了。

游戏卡住的时候，我们知道可能是网络不好、电脑带不动，或者等一会儿就能恢复。人生卡住的时候，却没有一个进度条告诉你还要等多久，也没有一键重启。

我现在也没有什么漂亮的答案。写下这些，并不是因为我已经想通了，而是因为我还没想通。

也许过一段时间再回头看，眼前的一些担心会变得没那么大；也许不会。但至少今天，我想先承认：我确实卡住了。我担心毕业，也担心未来；我想努力，只是暂时还不知道该往哪个方向用力。

如果你最近也有类似的感觉，希望你读到这里的时候，能知道还有人和你一样。

## English version: Stuck

Lately, I keep hearing a word in Chinese: **ka le**. It means something has frozen, lagged, or gotten stuck.

Your computer takes forever to respond. You click again and again, but nothing happens. It’s stuck. You’re playing a game, and your character suddenly stops moving. You press the buttons, but the screen doesn’t change. It’s stuck, too.

I used to think of the word as something you’d say about a computer or a game. Now it feels like a good way to describe people, too. You can still move around and get through your day, but somehow you don’t know where to go next.

That’s how I’ve been feeling lately.

### Everyone seems to be running toward AI

These days, it’s hard to talk about technology, jobs, or the future without talking about AI. New models keep coming out. Every time I open my phone, there’s news about another big investment or another product update. It feels like the world is speeding up.

But the world moving faster doesn’t mean I know what I should do.

Looking at the job market this year, I get the impression that semiconductors, materials, and some hardware fields are getting more attention. Companies are building AI data centers, and they need GPUs, chips, memory, storage, servers, networking equipment, and electricity. A lot of money is going in that direction, so some hardware companies are benefiting.

Of course, that doesn’t mean every hardware job is easy to get, or that software companies spend all their money on equipment. It’s just a trend I’ve noticed in the news and in conversations around me. And when so much money and so many resources are going into computing power, I can’t help wondering how many opportunities are left elsewhere.

### AI is everywhere, but where are the new opportunities?

I use AI myself, especially when I’m coding. It helps me look into problems, write code, and fix small tools. Things that used to take half a day can sometimes turn into a working first version much faster now.

But outside of those uses, I still feel that many businesses haven’t found a clear, mature way to use AI. I don’t mean there are no real applications. I just haven’t seen that many things that make me think, “We couldn’t even live this way before.”

When the internet first appeared, maybe it was mostly about putting information online. It was a bit like a digital phone book: you could look up a restaurant’s phone number or find someone’s contact information. Later came online shopping, food delivery, ride-hailing, digital banking, and online entertainment. They changed daily life and created new businesses and jobs.

Will AI follow a similar path? I don’t know. Maybe it’s still early, and a lot of those changes haven’t happened yet. But from where I’m standing, the spending on hardware and computing power is already huge, while the new kinds of businesses are still harder to see. That gap makes me uneasy.

### Faster doesn’t mean better

I have another strong feeling about AI: right now, it mostly makes work faster. I don’t think it makes the quality of my work better. At least, that’s been my own experience.

Take coding. It can give me an answer quickly. But I still have to check whether that answer fits my problem, whether there are hidden mistakes, and whether the code will hold up over time. AI can give me something that looks decent in less time. That doesn’t mean the result is actually good.

The same goes for writing. It’s easy to generate a long passage in a few seconds. But does it contain a real observation? Is the judgment sound? Is it what the writer actually wanted to say? Those are different questions.

I’m not saying AI is useless. I find it useful every day. I just think people sometimes make it sound smarter than it really is, as if it understands everything and can do anything. Use it long enough, and you’ll see it confidently give wrong answers or make mistakes in surprisingly simple situations. It can do a lot, but it has clear limits. I think there’s some hype and some bubble in all of this, though I don’t know how big the bubble is.

### What really has me stuck is my own next step

After talking about the industry, I always come back to myself.

I’m a student. Graduation is right in front of me, and finding a job comes after that. Some of my advisor’s decisions, some choices I’ve made, and the limits of my own abilities have all come together. It’s hard to point to one thing and say, “That’s the reason I’m here.”

I worry about whether I’ll graduate on time. And when I think about what comes after graduation, I don’t know what to do next. Should I stay in my current field or try something different? Should I prepare for an internship, work on the basics, or focus on my research first? Every option seems reasonable, and every option takes time.

The hardest part isn’t being busy. It’s not knowing where to put my effort.

Sometimes I tell myself, “If you’re worried, just work harder.” But then I sit down and ask: Work harder at what? Will the thing I’m doing today actually help me move forward? If I’m not even sure about the direction, will all that effort just take me in a circle?

Then the day is over. I’ve done a few things, but I don’t feel much more settled. At night, I lie in bed thinking about my paper, graduation, jobs, and all the questions I can’t answer yet. The next morning, I wake up and have to face them again.

### When you’re stuck

I think that’s what **ka le** means to me now.

On the surface, life goes on. I still have things to do every day. If someone asks, I can probably tell them what I’ve been busy with. But I know there are questions hanging over me. I’ve thought about them for a long time, and I still don’t know how to solve them. It looks like I’m moving forward, but inside, I feel like I’m out of ideas.

When a game freezes, maybe the internet is slow, maybe the computer can’t keep up, or maybe you just need to wait a little. When life gets stuck, there’s no progress bar telling you how long it will take, and there’s no button to restart everything.

I don’t have a neat answer right now. I’m writing this because I haven’t figured it out, not because I have.

Maybe, when I look back later, some of these worries won’t seem so big. Maybe they still will. But today, I want to admit that I feel stuck. I’m worried about graduation and about the future. I want to work hard; I just don’t know which direction to put that effort in yet.

If you’ve been feeling something similar lately, I hope this reminds you that you’re not the only one.
